\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied what happens to a privacy-preserving inference pipeline when the entity a
question asks about is also the entity that must be protected. Both standard remedies fail
there, for opposite reasons: redaction removes the referent the reader needs, and redundancy
ordering removes the record that carries the answer at close to chance, because the score
ranks units by self-similarity and is blind to which record the question is about.
\textsc{DARK} resolves the conflict by deciding the two axes per unit and giving the
sensitive-and-relevant cell the one operation neither remedy provides: the unit stays, its
values are aliased, and the same map is written into the question. Relevance is read from the
question---the record block that mentions the queried entity---so the pipeline needs neither a
reference answer nor training.

Across a 112-case benchmark over six public datasets, \textsc{DARK} keeps $48.6\%$ of the
tokens at zero prompt-level leakage. On the $2{,}728$-case extension the privacy result is the
stronger one: $0\%$ of protected values remain recoverable where the strongest query-aware
compressor leaves $98.1\%$, and a model asked to extract them recovers $36.4\%$ from the
uncompressed prompt and none from ours. The utility result depends on which question is asked, and that
split is the finding rather than a qualification. On the $1{,}228$ questions that name a record
identifier, \textsc{DARK} leads every compressor by $0.12$ to $0.42$ and matches the uncompressed
prompt at half its length. On the $1{,}500$ that name a protected account it scores below the
compressors, every one of which leaks on $89.5$--$100\%$ of those cases. Aliasing makes a
compressor safe and does not make it adequate: Quito-X on the aliased passage costs $0.0371$ F1
rather than gaining any. The 2026 rule-based compressor we re-run needs no model at all, yet costs
more tokens than we spend and still leaks in every case, because its rules delete the record syntax
the question depends on.

Crossing the two decisions isolates which of them carries the result. Aliasing is worth $+0.157$
and $+0.179$ at the two granularities, keeping the whole block $+0.079$ and $+0.057$ under the two
operations, and the interaction is small and positive. The operation is worth two to three times
the region. That ratio is the practical summary of this work: getting the surviving region right
is a precondition, and choosing the operation is what makes the task possible.

Two directions follow directly from the evidence. The first is to make the relevant region cover
the answer instead of relying on the fallback window: the two-hop variant shows that when the answer
lies outside it, recovery falls to the refill order, and widening the region is the configuration to
test next. The second is a guarantee that survives an adversary holding the alias map. Both extend
the same construction rather than replacing it.
