  \textbf{Fig.~1.} Why \textsc{DARK} matters. The question names exactly the value that must
not be sent, so redaction removes the handle the reader needs while compression forwards the
value it must hide; both destroy the task, for opposite reasons. (I)~The value to hide and the
answer live in the same record, which is why one cannot be removed without the other.
(II)~The two conventional choices fail in different
\emph{directions}, not by different amounts: masking keeps the task but loses the handle, and
the strongest compressor keeps the handle and forwards the value. (III)~The rule: each unit is
labelled on sensitivity and on relevance and the pair selects one of four operations---delete,
alias, keep or refill. The starred cell is the one no conventional remedy uses, and the only one
that removes the value while leaving the record addressable. (IV)~\textsc{DARK} reaches
$0.4937$ against that compressor's $0.4831$, a difference this experiment does not resolve, at
$0\%$ measured leakage against $98.1\%$. Leakage is protected-value recoverability under the
published pattern set, not a claim that the prompt is safe under every attack. (V)~The gain
tracks the record layout and is not uniform across sources. All numbers are from the
$2{,}728$-case extension at $r=0.50$.\par}%
